# Expressive Copy Layer

Use this layer after JD compliance gates and JD copy rules when a marketing surface needs more vivid wording. It provides style judgment, reader emotion, hook strength, information hierarchy, and anti-template review for eligible marketing copy.

Do not use it to replace JD 16.0 tone or commerce evidence rules.

## When To Use

Use for:

- campaign themes;
- venue head images;
- operation banners;
- channel cards;
- brand proposal lines;
- visual headline exploration;
- marketing copy that the user says feels too flat.

Skip for:

- payment, logistics, refund, after-sales, customer service, error, Toast, form validation;
- rights rules, legal disclaimers, service terms, settlement-page wording;
- sensitive product, price, rank, effect, service, or qualification claims before evidence is checked.

## Priority

Apply in this order:

1. Evidence and compliance.
2. JD tone: simple, reliable, friendly.
3. Touchpoint role and user task.
4. Clear user benefit.
5. Expressive hook and memorability.

If vividness weakens clarity or trust, choose clarity.

## Expression Diagnosis

Before rewriting, identify:

- content type: direct promotion, scene solution, seasonal activity, benefits explanation, brand trust, product discovery, proposal rationale;
- communication goal: click, understand, claim, browse, buy, trust, approve;
- reader emotion: save money, avoid effort, feel reassured, choose faster, find the right thing, enjoy the season, feel accompanied;
- information density: low, medium, high;
- risk level: low marketing risk, price/right/service risk, product/category risk.

## Output Modes

Generate different directions instead of synonym swaps:

- Safe direct: clearest and most rule-stable.
- Scene vivid: enters through a user scene or seasonal task.
- Click stronger: has a sharper hook while staying true.
- Brand steadier: reinforces JD reliability, service, or shopping confidence.
- Proposal persuasive: explains why the line fits the strategy and touchpoint.

Use 3-5 modes only when the brief supports them.

## Hook Mechanisms

Use these mechanisms inside JD boundaries:

- Scene entry: connect the benefit to a real shopping moment.
  - Example pattern: `<season/task>, <benefit> is easier to find`.
- Action trigger: turn the benefit into a clear next action.
  - Example pattern: `先 <action>, 再 <outcome>`.
- Concrete benefit: replace vague value with what the user gets.
  - Example pattern: `<rule-backed benefit>, <user outcome>`.
- Soft contrast: make the choice clearer without attacking or exaggerating.
  - Example pattern: `不用到处比，来这里按需挑`.
- Trust cue: use rule, settlement, service, or official activity language where evidence exists.
  - Example pattern: `<benefit>, 以活动页/结算页为准`.

## Avoid

- unsupported lowest-price, first, only, must-buy, all-network, best-ever, 100%, guaranteed language;
- anxiety pressure such as missed means loss, regret for a year, hurry or lose;
- cheap internet slang, overdrama, exaggerated punctuation, or over-cute tone;
- poetic lines that hide the offer;
- generic templates such as good goods are all here, benefits are coming, surprise never stops, quality life, selected goods;
- making JD sound like a personal influencer account unless the surface explicitly asks for social-style content.

## Rewrite Loop

1. Write a plain JD-safe base line.
2. Pick one reader emotion and one hook mechanism.
3. Rewrite into 3-5 differentiated directions.
4. Remove any option that needs missing evidence.
5. Check whether the main benefit is understandable in about 3 seconds.
6. Recommend one option and explain why it fits JD.
